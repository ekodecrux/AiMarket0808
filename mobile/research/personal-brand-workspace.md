# Personal Brand Workspace Research

LinkedIn describes a profile as a professional landing page for managing a personal brand. Its guidance emphasizes a professional photo and background image, a headline that can express expertise or personality, an About section that explains mission, motivation, and skills, relevant experience and media samples, relevant skills, recommendations, and profile visibility controls. [1]

The Personal Brand workspace should therefore help an individual prepare and review these elements, then use their approved positioning as reusable operating knowledge for thought leadership and relationship planning. It must not encourage automated connection requests, automated engagement, or password sharing. Publishing or relationship activity should remain a user-reviewed draft unless and until a provider-approved OAuth integration explicitly allows a selected action.

## Product model

| Workspace lane | Plain-language outcome | User-controlled action |
|---|---|---|
| Position | A clear answer to “who do I help and why listen?” | Save personal positioning statement |
| Profile | A checklist for headline, About, proof, skills, and social proof | Copy approved text into the user’s own profile |
| Thought leadership | A weekly set of useful points of view and content prompts | Review, edit, and publish selected drafts |
| Relationships | A focused list of people and value-led reasons to reconnect | Draft—not send—personalized outreach |
| Scorecard | Goals for profile quality, publishing consistency, conversations, and opportunities | Review progress without vanity-metric promises |
